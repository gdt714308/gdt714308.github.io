About

Student, outdoorsman, and utilitarian seeking to align the global economy with planetary health and human well-being.

The theory

Ecology and economics are two ends of one framework. Every economy runs on natural capital: water, soil, forests, fisheries, and a stable climate. Yet most markets still price it poorly or not at all.

People often ask me whether more climate data can accomplish anything, given how many warnings have already been ignored. I think the problem was never the amount of information. It was the form. Global, long-horizon estimates are easy to discount. A causally identified cost for a specific place is not. It turns "society bears a cost" into "this county, this water district, this budget bears a cost," and it gives insurers, budget offices, regulators, courts, and investors a number to act on. Once a cost has a real price, the people who pay it have leverage to change the system.

The practice

Climate action is short on operators: people who change systems through incentives backed by strong science. That is the role I am building toward, and my work follows the chain from measurement to action.

  1. MeasureSensor networks and machine learning track ecosystems continuously, not once a decade.
  2. ModelThose data feed climate and economic models at local scale, like our 30-year projection of Chicago's heat costs.
  3. PriceTheseus Capital tests whether markets price climate risk and natural capital correctly.
  4. ActReal prices give stakeholders, especially in developing economies, the leverage to manage energy and natural capital.

Theseus Capital

Founder · The natural capital misalignment hypothesis

An investment hypothesis built on the gap between what markets charge and what the physical world can sustain.

  • Major commodities, real estate markets, and municipal bonds still do not account for climate risk or natural capital.
  • Developing an investment hypothesis that tracks this misalignment, market by market.
  • Aiming to show that markets will converge on physical realities, and that pricing those realities correctly is immensely valuable.

UChicago Climate Case Competition

Illinois clean energy grid transition

Our team modeled a transition of the Illinois grid that reaches 95.1% clean electricity by 2045, and designed a three-part plan to pay for it.

  • An externality tax that prices the damage from fossil generation.
  • Clean power purchase agreements as a condition for new data centers.
  • Green bonds paired with federal grants.
